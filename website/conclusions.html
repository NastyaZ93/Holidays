Conclusions

Toys win, and the season starts in mid-October

Toys gain most from the holiday season, and holiday shopping starts about 10 weeks before Christmas for the season in general and about 6 weeks before for toys.

Our three hypotheses

# We expected that… Result
H1 November and December are the biggest spending peak of the year ✔ Confirmed
H2 Gift categories drive the peak, not everyday shopping ✔ Confirmed
H3 People start searching weeks before they buy ◐ Partly Confirmed for toys and Christmas; other categories peak at Black Friday

Which categories gain most?

Toys (+72% in November–December), then clothing (+42%) and electronics (+36%). Groceries barely move (+7%), so the peak is about gifts, not everyday shopping.

How early does holiday shopping start?

Interest in Christmas rises from mid-October. Toy searches rise from early November and peak in the second week of December. Clothing and electronics are Black Friday categories: people search for them when the deals come, at the end of November.

Our recommendations

  1. Have holiday stock and content ready by mid-October, before searches start to rise.
  2. Launch toy campaigns in early November and keep them running until the second week of December.
  3. Put the clothing and electronics budget into Black Friday week. That is when shoppers look for these categories.
  4. Plan drinks and party campaigns for the week between Christmas and New Year.
  5. Don't move grocery budget into the holidays. Grocery spending and searches hardly change.

Limitations

  • Google Trends shows relative search interest, not purchases or numbers of people.
  • Broad words like "books" or "clothing" also catch searches that have nothing to do with the holidays.
  • Sales are in current dollars, so part of the yearly growth is inflation.
  • 2020 was a Covid year. Toy search timing in 2020 matched the other years, but its sales figures may be unusual.
  • National monthly data cannot show holidays that move each year, such as Eid, Diwali or Lunar New Year.
  • We show patterns that coincide with the holidays, not proof of what causes them.
